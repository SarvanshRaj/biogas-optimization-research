## 2. Objective, users, decision context

**Objective.** A computational follow-up to an earlier *mechanical* fermentation prototype. The earlier
prototype was built and tested with real food waste during the Sakura Science Exchange Programme 2025;
the programme announced results before the team reached the software stage. **No simulation, software, or
trained model existed in that programme.** This project starts fresh, on synthetic data, and its outputs
are never evidence about that or any other physical system.

**What the project can legitimately deliver**
1. A transparent simulator whose assumptions are visible and adjustable.
2. An honest benchmark of how five standard regression models behave on that simulator under a
   leakage-safe, scenario-grouped split.
3. A constrained search over simulated settings, reported with its fragility, not as a recipe.
4. A reproducible repository and a report a Class XII student can defend line by line.

**What it cannot deliver, by construction**
- Any estimate of methane yield from real food waste.
- Any evidence for or against *Aspergillus oryzae* pre-treatment as a real process.
- Any model accuracy claim transferable to a physical digester.

**Core circularity, stated up front.** The models are trained on data generated by an equation I wrote.
Their scores measure how well they recover *that equation* under noise, nothing more. Model ranking on
synthetic data can differ completely from ranking on real measurements. This sentence must appear in the
README, in report §9, and alongside every metrics artifact.

## 7. Priors and the steelman rule

Priors are mine (stated before seeing any evidence), stated before Phase 1 evidence. Qualitative where precision
would be fake.

| # | Proposition | Prior | Rationale | Posterior (Phase 5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P-A | Retrievable sources support a **real, food-waste-specific** methane-yield benefit of *A. oryzae* pre-treatment. | 0.30 (low–moderate) | Fungal pre-treatment evidence is dominated by lignocellulosic substrates; food waste is already highly degradable, so the headroom is small and effects, where reported, are often modest or substrate-specific. ||
| P-B | Methane potential depends nonlinearly on lipid fraction, with clear inhibition above a threshold. | 0.75 (moderate–high) | Lipid/LCFA inhibition is a well-reported mechanism in AD literature; the numeric threshold is what requires sourcing. ||
| P-C | A model ranking obtained on synthetic data carries information about ranking on real digesters. | **0.15 (low)** | The data are generated by a closed-form equation the models are asked to recover; this tests the learners, not the biology. This prior is deliberately hostile to the project's own most attractive claim. ||
| P-D | The optimiser's recommended simulated setting will remain best under ±5 % perturbation of its inputs. | 0.55 (uncertain) | Optimisers exploit sharp ridges in synthetic response surfaces; broad optima are not guaranteed. ||

**Steelman rule.** Phase 2 must steelman the best case *against* the project's most
attractive narratives, that pre-treatment helps, that the simulator's structure is adequate, and that
synthetic benchmarks are informative. Phase 5 must report whichever of those survived, including
disconfirming evidence, even where it weakens the project's story.

## 8. Falsifiers and the action each triggers

| ID | Falsifier | Action if triggered |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Phase 1 finds **no** retrievable source linking *A. oryzae* (or comparable fungal pre-treatment) to food-waste methane yield. | Report that plainly; model pre-treatment as a pure signed sensitivity knob spanning harm↔benefit with a default neutral value, labelled **ASSUMPTION FOR SIMULATION**; state that the simulation cannot test the hypothesis. Update P-A posterior downward. |
| F2 | Sources indicate pre-treatment *reduces* methane potential in some food-waste cases (mass loss, acidification). | Widen the factor range symmetrically and include a documented harmful regime in scenarios; report both directions. |
| F3 | The chosen ground truth is effectively linear or additive: Ridge achieves ≥ 95 % of the best tree model's test R² (mean over seeds). | Declare the comparison uninformative for that structure; revise the equation (documented as an **AD_HOC** change with before/after results) or report the degenerate finding honestly. Never inflate nonlinearity to make models look interesting. |
| F4 | Any scenario group appears in more than one split, or preprocessing is fit on data outside the training fold. | Pipeline fails its own test suite; block Phase 4; fix before reporting anything. |
| F5 | The optimiser's recommendation is not robust: perturbation (±5 %) changes the recommended region, or the recomputed yield falls below the baseline mixture. | Report the recommendation as fragile/artefactual; do not present any "best recipe"; report the baseline comparison instead. |
| F6 | Simulated outputs violate physical bounds (negative yield, methane fraction > 1, mass-balance breach). | Simulator bug; block and fix. |
| F7 | Reported metric depends materially on seed choice (mean ± spread overlapping the dummy baseline's spread). | Report that the apparent skill is seed-dependent and not distinguishable from the baseline; do not claim the model works. |

## 11. Process decisions (P1–P10)

Each decision states what it is, why it is defensible, and what would prove it wrong.

**P1: Split.** Scenarios are the splitting unit. One scenario = one feedstock/process combination with 8
batch replicates that share it. Split by `scenario_id`: 70 % train / 15 % validation / 15 % test, with a
**further held-out scenario family** (e.g. the highest-lipid regime) reserved as an out-of-distribution
probe. *Why:* replicates of one scenario are near-duplicates; scattering them across folds would inflate
scores and test memorisation, not generalisation. *Disproved by:* any group overlap (F4) or a test-set
score that collapses under the OOD probe while train-like scores stay high.

**P2: Rows.** 500 scenarios × 8 replicates = 4,000 rows; 5 seeds over the whole generate→split→fit→score
path. *Why:* enough rows for five models to be fitted and diagnosed on 2 vCPU without GPU, with the
pipeline running end-to-end in minutes; this is a runtime trade-off and is **not** a power calculation.

**P3: Models.** Exactly the five required: `DummyRegressor`, `Ridge`, `RandomForestRegressor`,
scikit-learn's `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`, and a small `MLPRegressor`. *Why:* the study fixed
these; adding more would inflate the comparison without informing it. *Disproved by:* a model that cannot
be trained reproducibly on this hardware, which will be reported, not hidden.

**P4: Diagnostics.** Multi-seed mean ± standard deviation for R², MAE, RMSE; intervals reported as
descriptive spread over 5 seeds only (t-intervals are shown solely if labelled "descriptive, n = 5,
normality unverified"). Residual and predicted-vs-true plots per model per representative seed. Feature
importance described as *properties of the simulator*, never as biological causality.

**P5: Target.** Dimensionless `simulated_yield_index` in [0, 100] by default, clamped, with a documented
monotone map from the internal physical-scale-free potential. Physical units only if Phase 1 supplies a
source justifying a scale, and then labelled "simulated, scale adopted from <source>".

**P6: Pre-treatment representation.** `pretreatment_net_effect = availability_gain − degradable_mass_loss`,
with the sampled range spanning harmful (< 1), neutral (= 1) and beneficial (> 1) regimes, plus a stated
"no-effect" band. Never hard-coded as an improvement (answers Q2; falsifiers F1/F2).

**P7: Optimiser.** Maximise the simulated outcome subject to: fractions in [0.02, 0.90] and summing to 1;
process settings inside the sourced or assumption-labelled ranges; and a **feasibility/stability
constraint** (simulated inhibition indicator below the level where the simulator reports instability).
Compare against the equal-thirds baseline at mid-range settings; perturb the recommendation by ±5 % on
continuous inputs and by round-robin fraction shifts; report the fraction of perturbations that stay
inside the recommended region. *Labelled throughout:* an optimiser finds the best point in *my equation*;
it is not a food-waste recipe, and its exploits of the equation are reported as artefacts.

**P8: Static checks and coverage.** ruff (format/lint) and mypy where practical; pytest + pytest-cov for
statement **and** branch coverage of the core modules (`simulate_data`, validation, splitting/
preprocessing, `train_models`, `evaluate`, `optimize`, CLI); 100 % statement+branch target on core logic
with changed-line coverage reported; mutation testing attempted (mutmut) and its score reported honestly
if it runs, otherwise **RUN-BLOCKED** with the exact command. *Disproved by:* an uncovered branch, which
is a blocker, not a footnote.

**P9: Provenance schema.** Every numeric parameter carries:
`name · value_or_range · unit · source_class(direct|analogous|assumption) · citation_id · applicability_note · sensitivity_range`.
`source_class = assumption` renders as **ASSUMPTION FOR SIMULATION** in every output.

**P10: Change protocol after freeze.** Post-Phase-2 changes are permitted only with: a stated reason, a
label **AD_HOC**, before/after results, and a line in the notebook's phase log. Silent post-hoc tuning of
the ground truth is treated as a research-integrity failure (and is what F3 guards against).
